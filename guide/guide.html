How it works

One order, followed from the moment a customer asks for it to the moment they pay.

Order tracker for sourcing businesses
The problem

Where is my order?

A sourcing business runs on messages. Customers ask for things in DMs, sizes and budgets live in a chat history, and the same question arrives every few days: where is my order?

Which means four things go wrong, quietly and constantly:

  • You answer the same question all week. Every customer, every few days, for every item.
  • You retype everything. A request arrives as a photo and a paragraph; you copy it somewhere by hand, or you don't and you forget it.
  • You shop from memory. Standing in a shop abroad, trying to remember who wanted size 38 and what they were willing to spend.
  • You don't really know what you made. Cost here, price there, a deposit taken in cash. The number at the end of a trip is a guess.

This is one app that removes all four.

It has two doors: one for you, one for each customer. You get everything — prices, costs, profit. They get their own order and nothing else. The pages that follow walk through a single order from both sides.

Step 1

A customer asks for something

You put one link in your Instagram bio. Anyone can fill it in — name, WhatsApp number, what they want, their size, their budget, and a photo.

Nothing here becomes a customer or an order. It waits for you.

The photo matters more than it sounds. A written description of a bag is never enough to find the right bag; the picture they already have on their phone is.

Step 2

It arrives in your inbox

Requests collect in one place with a badge showing how many are waiting. No scrolling back through DMs to find the one you meant to answer.

You either take it or you don't. Rejecting keeps the record without creating anything.

Step 3

Approve, in one tap

Approving creates the customer, creates the order, and attaches their photo. You never retype a word of it.

People write the same Lebanese number as +961 71 … and 71 …. The app spots that they match and offers to add the order to the existing customer rather than making a second one — but it asks, because two different people can share a first name.

The app never messages a customer on its own. It opens WhatsApp with the text ready — you read it, change it, send it or don't.

Step 4

Everything you still have to find

One screen, every customer, oldest request first. Their photo, their size, their budget. This is the screen you open walking into a shop.

Found and Bought are full-width buttons. A tapped item doesn't vanish — it dims and offers Undo, so a wrong tap while moving fast costs nothing.

Step 5

You find it, and you price it

Enter what you paid and what you're charging. Profit appears as you type, before you commit to the number.

Step 6

Your customer can just look

Every customer gets a private link. No account, no password, no app to install. They open it whenever they wonder, instead of messaging you.

They see the photo of what you actually found — so "is this the right one?" is answered before it's asked.

Step 7

They pay

Your account number and the exact amount, each copied with one tap. Nobody retypes a number out of a paragraph and gets it wrong.

The amount shown is what's actually left after any deposit. When it reaches zero the whole section disappears, because there's nothing left to do.

Step 8

What your customer never sees

This is the part that matters most, and it is worth being precise about.

Your cost and your profit are not hidden by the screen. They are never sent to the customer's phone at all.

The customer's page cannot read the database. It can call exactly one function, which returns a fixed list of things: description, size, status, price, deposit, photo. Cost is not on that list, so there is nothing to find — not in the page, not in the network traffic, not by anyone technical who goes looking.

Practically: you can hand a customer their link without a second thought, and you can send it in a group chat without checking what's on it.

Step 9

You know exactly what you made

Billed, cost, profit, deposits collected, balance still outstanding — over any dates you choose, filtered by any status.

Step 10

Finished orders get out of the way

When an order is delivered and paid, you close it. It disappears from the customer's link, so a returning customer opens it and sees only what they have on order now.

Closed orders stay in your dashboard, your profit and your exports. They were real sales. They just stop cluttering the screen.

In short

What you get

  • Your own app, in your name and your colours, at your own address.
  • Your own database. Nothing shared with any other business.
  • A private link for every customer — no accounts, no passwords, nothing to install.
  • A request link for your Instagram bio.
  • Cost and profit protected at the database, not by the screen.
  • Arabic and English, switchable on every page.
  • Installs on your phone like a normal app.
  • Excel and PDF reports whenever your accountant asks.
