# The demo — 6 minutes

For selling a one-off build to another Instagram sourcing business.

**Bring two devices.** Laptop for your side, phone for the customer's side. The
whole sale turns on them seeing both at once.

Open beforehand, so nothing loads in front of them:

- Laptop: signed in at `/admin`, sitting on **Dashboard**
- Phone: one customer's link already open, screen unlocked
- A second browser tab on `/request`

---

## 0 · Before you open anything (30 seconds)

Do not start with the app. Start with their day.

> "Can I ask — how many times a week does someone message you asking where
> their order is?"

Let them answer. Then:

> "And when you're in Paris, how do you remember who wanted what size?"

Now they are describing the problem in their own words. Everything you show
next is an answer to something they just said.

---

## 1 · Your side (90 seconds)

**Dashboard.** Don't explain it. Point at three numbers.

> "Total billed. Total cost. Profit. Any date range I want."

**Clients → open one → open an item.**

> "This is one order. I paid 285. I charge 460. That's my 175 — right there,
> I never work it out on paper."

Pause on the amber strip that says **Hidden from client**.

---

## 2 · The moment that sells it (60 seconds)

Pick up your phone. Hand it to them.

> "That's the same order. This is what *their* customer sees."

Let them scroll. Then say the important sentence:

> "Look what isn't there. No cost. No profit. And it's not hidden by the
> screen — those numbers are never sent to the customer's phone at all.
> Even if they knew how to look, there's nothing to find."

This is the part they repeat to their business partner afterwards. Do not rush
it, and do not add anything to it.

---

## 3 · The trip (60 seconds)

**To source** on the laptop, but describe the phone.

> "This is everything I still have to find, for every customer, oldest first.
> Their photo, their size, their budget. I open this walking into a shop —
> one hand, one thumb. Found. Bought. Done."

If they do buying trips, this lands harder than anything else. Watch their face
here; if they lean in, spend an extra thirty seconds.

---

## 4 · Where the work disappears (90 seconds)

**Requests tab** — point at the green badge.

> "This is a link in my Instagram bio. They fill it in with a photo, it lands
> here. I press Approve — that's it, they're a customer, the order exists, the
> photo is already attached. I never retype anything from a DM again."

Approve the pending one from *Maya Khoury* — it will offer to attach to the
existing customer rather than making a duplicate.

> "It even noticed she's already a customer, because the number matches."

Then the green strip appears:

> "And it offers to message her that it's approved. Written already. I read it,
> I press send. The app never messages my customers on its own — that stays
> mine."

---

## 5 · Money and paperwork (45 seconds)

Back to **Dashboard** → pick a status → **Download Excel**, then **Save as PDF**.

> "Filtered however I want, out to Excel or a PDF, in two clicks. That's my
> accountant's problem solved too."

---

## 6 · Close

> "It's yours, in your name, your colours, your own database. Nothing shared
> with anyone. About a week."

Then stop talking. Let them ask the price.

---

## The three questions they will ask

**"Is my data safe / who else can see it?"**
> "Your own database, in your name. I don't share it with anyone else's copy.
> Your customers can only ever see their own order — that's enforced in the
> database, not just the screen."

**"What if I want to change something later?"**
> Be honest. Say what a change costs and how long it takes. Don't promise
> unlimited free changes — that's how these deals go bad.

**"Can it do [thing]?"**
> If it can't, say so, and say what it would take. A confident "not today, that
> would be about two days' work" sells better than a vague yes.

---

## Do not do these

- **Do not demo the real live app.** They would see the real business's
  customers, prices and margins. Use only this demo copy.
- **Do not read out the feature list.** Everything above is a story about one
  working day. The moment it becomes a list, you have lost the room.
- **Do not discount when they hesitate.** Remove features instead, and say
  which ones. A cheaper price teaches them the first number was invented.
